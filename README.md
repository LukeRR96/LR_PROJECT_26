# Groundwater Nitrogen Plume Modeling Using ArcNLET

This project uses ArcNLET to simulate groundwater nitrogen transport from septic systems and evaluate the effectiveness of aerobic treatment units (ATUs) in reducing plume concentrations.

## Project Overview

Septic systems are a major source of nitrogen to groundwater, particularly in coastal regions with shallow water tables. This project models nitrogen transport within the Stroud Creek watershed in North Fort Myers, Florida to assess plume behavior under different treatment scenarios.

Two scenarios were evaluated:
- Conventional septic systems
- Aerobic Treatment Units (ATUs) with reduced nitrogen input

The goal of this project is to quantify how nitrogen concentrations and plume extent change under improved treatment conditions.

---

## Methodology

### ArcNLET Modeling

ArcNLET was used to simulate groundwater flow and nitrogen transport using:

- Septic systems as point sources
- Groundwater flow direction and velocity rasters
- Transport parameters including porosity and dispersivity
- Steady-state transport conditions

The model generates plume rasters representing groundwater nitrate-nitrogen (NO3-N) concentrations.

---

### Data Processing

Model outputs were:
- Exported as raster datasets
- Converted to tabular data (CSV format)
- Used for further statistical analysis and visualization

---

## Study Area

- Stroud Creek Watershed
- North Fort Myers, Florida
- Coastal system with shallow groundwater conditions and widespread septic system use

---

## Key Findings

- Conventional systems produce larger and higher concentration nitrogen plumes
- ATUs significantly reduce plume magnitude and spatial extent
- Nitrogen concentrations shift downward across the entire plume distribution
- Exceedance of the 10 mg/L drinking water standard is substantially reduced under ATU conditions
